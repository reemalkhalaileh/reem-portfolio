Featured research

Re‑engineering the substrate specificity of RuBisCO

MSc thesis · Morii Laboratory (Profs. Takashi Morii and Eiji Nakata), Institute of Advanced Energy, Kyoto University · 2023–2025

The question

RuBisCO is the enzyme through which most carbon enters the biosphere, and it is famously slow and imprecise. I asked whether its enzyme–substrate interface could be rationally redesigned to shift its specificity, working with the Type III RuBisCO of the archaeon Thermococcus kodakarensis, a hyperthermophile from Kodakara Island, Japan, whose RuBisCO stays folded and active at 85 °C.

Inside the active site

What I built

A computational screening workflow that uses structure prediction and molecular docking to rank candidate enzyme variants and substrate analogues before any bench work, so that only the most promising designs go on to experiments. Steps 1–2 are the workflow I designed; steps 3–4 are the established bench protocols I used to test it. The glowing molecule stands for a substrate analogue bound in the pocket.

The lab’s computational strength was in DNA nanotechnology, so I taught myself AlphaFold2 and AutoDock Vina to bring structure‑based design to the project.

01 · Predict · in silico

Model enzyme variants with AlphaFold2

Every candidate mutation becomes a structure before it becomes a plasmid.

02 · Dock & rank · in silico

Score variants and substrate analogues with AutoDock Vina

Designs are ranked by how they hold the substrate in the pocket. Poses are benchmarked against the crystal structure before the ranking is trusted.

03 · Build · at the bench

Site‑directed mutagenesis, expression and purification

A focused set of designs, not a random library, is built and purified in multiple steps (heat treatment, anion‑exchange and size‑exclusion chromatography).

04 · Test · at the bench

Measure activity with NADH‑coupled enzyme assays

The prediction meets the bench.

Outcome

Six designed variants were built and tested alongside the wild type through mutagenesis, purification and activity assays. The work was presented at three conferences in Japan, including an oral talk at the 105th Annual Meeting of the Chemical Society of Japan.
